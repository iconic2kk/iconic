Alamo Region Manufacturing Compact

Charter and Breadth Fund terms · consolidated text incorporating the amendments of 14 November 2023

Scenario document authored for this exercise. The Compact, its board, secretariat and finance office are fictional. The member counties are real, and the employer counts this charter refers to are the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' published Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages figures.

Article 1. Why the Compact exists

1.1 The Compact was formed in 2019 by the commissioners courts of the thirteen counties of the Alamo Area Council of Governments region, after a run of plant closures between 2015 and 2018 took several kinds of manufacturing out of the region altogether.

1.2 The region makes a great many different things. Some kinds of manufacturing are carried by hundreds of plants in and around San Antonio; others are carried by one or two plants in a rural county. When a kind of manufacturing leaves the region, the suppliers, skills and training that grew up around it tend to leave with it, and it rarely comes back.

1.3 The Compact's purpose is to keep the region's manufacturing broad: to stand behind the lines of manufacturing the region has kept, wherever in the region they are carried, so that the smaller lines are not crowded out by the larger ones.

Article 2. Members and board

2.1 The members are the counties of Atascosa, Bandera, Bexar, Comal, Frio, Gillespie, Guadalupe, Karnes, Kendall, Kerr, McMullen, Medina and Wilson. Each has been a member since the Compact was formed.

2.2 Each member's commissioners court appoints one director. The directors elect a chair for a two-year term. The board meets at least four times a year and always in November.

2.3 A quorum is seven directors. Decisions are taken by a simple majority of the directors present, except that an amendment to this charter needs the votes of ten directors.

2.4 The secretariat is provided by the chair's county under a services agreement renewed every two years. It keeps the Compact's records, prepares each round and gives the notices this charter requires. The finance office manages the Compact's accounts, its reserve and its planning projections.

Article 3. The Breadth Fund

3.1 The Breadth Fund is $5,000,000 in each round. It is met from members' annual contributions under Schedule 1 and from the state economic development grant the Compact receives each year. If the grant falls short, the board may draw the difference from the reserve before the round is confirmed.

3.2 The fund is spread across the member counties once a year, in a round. A county's part of the fund in a round is its draw. Rounds are named by the year in which the board confirms them; the first round was the 2020 round.

3.3 The fund follows the region's manufacturing rather than its headcount or its payroll. It stands behind the lines of manufacturing the region has kept, and each member's draw reflects the part the county plays in carrying them.

3.4 The secretariat works each round from the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages county files: for each member county, the annual average number of private establishments in each four-digit manufacturing industry of the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS 3111 to 3399), as BLS publishes it. Each round is worked from the published counts for the calendar year before the round, together with the counts for the year before that.

3.5 Draws are rounded to the whole dollar. The board confirms the round at its November meeting and the draws are paid in the following January.

3.6 Nothing in a round depends on a county's contribution under Schedule 1, its population, or the amount of grants it has made in earlier years.

Article 4. Notices and the transition reserve

4.1 If a member county's draw in a round is more than ten per cent below its draw in the round before, the secretariat gives the county written notice by 1 December of the year of the round.

4.2 A county that receives a notice is paid, from the transition reserve, half of the difference between its draw in the round before and its draw in the round, rounded to the whole dollar. The payment is made with the draw in January.

4.3 A transition payment is not part of the county's draw, and it is not counted when the next round's draw is compared with this one.

4.4 The finance office keeps the transition reserve at no less than $250,000 at the start of each round. The board tops it up from members' contributions when it falls below that level.

Article 5. What draws may be used for

5.1 A member county uses its draw for retention grants to manufacturing establishments in the county. Grants may meet the cost of equipment that keeps a production line in the county, training for the establishment's employees, utility connections and upgrades, and the cost of meeting new state or federal standards.

5.2 No single establishment may receive more than $150,000 from the Compact in any twelve months, counting grants from every member county.

5.3 A grant may not be used to move an establishment from one member county to another.

5.4 A county may carry up to a quarter of its draw into the following year. Any amount carried for longer, or above that limit, returns to the reserve.

Article 6. Reporting and audit

6.1 By 30 June each year a member county reports to the secretariat every grant it made from its draw in the previous year: the establishment, its industry, the amount and the purpose.

6.2 The Compact's accounts are audited each year by an independent auditor appointed by the board. The auditor may examine the secretariat's working for any round.

6.3 The secretariat keeps the round ledger, the published BLS counts it used for each round and its working for each round for at least seven years.

Article 7. Projections

7.1 To help members plan their grant programmes, the finance office may issue projections of draws before a round is confirmed.

7.2 A projection is for planning only. A county's draw is the amount the board confirms in November. The Compact does not make good any difference between a projection and a confirmed draw, except through a transition payment under Article 4.

Article 8. Conflicts of interest

8.1 A director who has a financial interest in an establishment that has applied for, or received, a grant from the director's county declares the interest to the board and takes no part in any decision about that establishment.

8.2 Officers of the secretariat and the finance office may not hold an interest in any establishment that receives a grant.

Article 9. Amendment and withdrawal

9.1 This charter may be amended by the votes of ten directors at a meeting called with at least thirty days' notice of the proposed amendment.

9.2 A member may withdraw at the end of any round by giving notice before 1 July of that year. A county that withdraws receives no draw in later rounds and its contributions already paid are not returned.
