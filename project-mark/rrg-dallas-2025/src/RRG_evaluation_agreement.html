Relocation and Retention Grant (RRG): evaluation agreement

Between the County Workforce Board and the RRG program office · signed 14 June 2024 · Annex A added 26 March 2026
Scenario document authored for this exercise. The program, the board and the office are fictional; the population figures referred to are the U.S. Census Bureau's published estimates.

1. The program

The RRG pays relocation grants to households moving to Dallas County, Texas, from elsewhere in the United States, and retention payments to resident households in their first three years in the county. It opened on 1 July 2024. Its first term ends on 30 June 2027.

2. What the board judges

The board judges the RRG on Dallas County's domestically sourced growth: the growth that comes from births, deaths and moves within the United States, per 1,000 residents. International migration is outside the program's reach and is left out.

3. Comparison

Dallas County is compared with the rest of Texas: the state's other counties, taken as one area.

4. Years

Years are Census estimate years, each running from one 1 July to the next. The program year is 1 July 2024 to 1 July 2025. The base year is the estimate year before it, and the check year is the estimate year before the base year.

5. Effect

The effect is the change in Dallas County's rate from the base year to the program year, less the change in the rest of Texas's rate between the same two years, in points per 1,000.

6. Parallel-movement check

The same calculation, taken from the check year to the base year, must lie within 1.50 points per 1,000 of zero. If it does not, the two areas were not moving together before the program began, and the board cannot credit the program with any effect.

7. Decision

If the parallel-movement check holds and the effect is at least 1.80 points per 1,000, the board extends the RRG for a second term, 1 July 2027 to 30 June 2030. Otherwise the RRG lapses at the end of its first term. The effect and the check are compared with these thresholds before any rounding.

8. Data

All three years are taken from the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 county estimates (co-est2025-alldata), as published.

Annex A: worked illustration (added 26 March 2026)

When Vintage 2025 was released, the office worked the board's calculation for the check year (1 July 2022 to 1 July 2023) so that members could see how the board's figures are formed. On Vintage 2025, Dallas County's domestically sourced growth in the check year was −6.2 per 1,000 and the rest of Texas's was +13.2 per 1,000, a gap of 19.5 points per 1,000 in the rest of Texas's favour.
